# Enzyme-quantum-tunnel-through-off-shell-molecular-pseudo-radiation
A paper that attempts to answer this problem:
"Can an approaching substrate meaningfully alter an enzyme's conformational probability distribution before binding, 
 or does the effect occur only once a transient encounter complex has formed?"

three evidence levels:
  a) established (enzyme tunneling, electrostatic steering, conformational ensembles).
  b) proposed (approach-dependent population bias toward tunneling-ready substates)
  c) speculative extension (coupling that microscopic state to transcriptional regulation).

The quick post-conclusion:
"the proposed effect must be fast enough to occur during molecular approach."

At physiological ionic strength, electrostatic interactions are strongly screened. 
A freely diffusing substrate may spend only nanoseconds near the enzyme before contact or escape, 
 whereas some protein conformational changes take microseconds or longer.

Abstract:

Quantum mechanical tunneling contributes measurably to selected enzyme-catalyzed hydrogen- and
electron-transfer reactions, while electrostatic steering, electrostatic preorganization, protein
dynamics, and conformational selection independently shape molecular recognition and catalytic
competence. This article proposes a unified but deliberately conservative extension of these
observations: an approaching substrate may, within the ordinary range of electrostatic and solventmediated interaction, perturb the population of a pre-existing enzyme conformational ensemble
before a long-lived Michaelis complex is formed. If particular substates shorten donor-acceptor
distances or reshape the local electrostatic environment, such an encounter-stage population bias
could increase the probability of reaching a tunneling-ready configuration. Tunneling itself remains
a short-range quantum event and does not transmit information ahead of the substrate or require
non-causal physics. A minimal statistical-mechanical model is formulated in which the substratedependent electric field shifts conformational free energies and the observed reaction rate is an
ensemble average of configuration-dependent tunneling rates. The hypothesis generates falsifiable
predictions involving ionic strength, charge-isosteric substrate analogues, distal electrostatic
mutations, time-resolved conformational probes, vibrational Stark spectroscopy, kinetic isotope
effects, and multiscale Brownian-dynamics/MD/QM-MM simulations. A more speculative extension
considers whether repeated microscopic catalytic-state changes could couple indirectly to
transcriptional regulation through established metabolite-sensing or signaling pathways. The
proposal therefore separates three levels of claim: established quantum-assisted catalysis, testable
pre-reactive ensemble gating, and speculative regulatory coupling
